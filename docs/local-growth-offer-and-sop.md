# Click & Mortar — Local Growth System
**Internal operating specification · Version 1.0 · October 8, 2026**

> **Positioning:** Click & Mortar helps established Western Colorado service businesses get found, earn trust, and make it easier for local customers to contact them.
>
> **Founder rule:** Sell a service we can actually deliver. Evidence before expansion; retention before scaling. This playbook defines the initial offer, not a promise of rankings or revenue.

## 1. Offer and qualifications

**Public name:** Click & Mortar Local Growth System  
**Founding rate:** $399/month, one eligible business location and one Google Business Profile.  
**Entry point:** Free Local Visibility Check, a brief public-information assessment with up to three observations and next steps.  
**Standard onboarding:** Included at the founding rate when there is an eligible, accessible existing profile and normal scope of work. Heavy remediation or one-time migration is a separate quote.  
**Billing/termination:** First payment before work starts. Exact recurring billing, cancellation notice, and fees must be established in the written service agreement. Do not publicly promise a contract term absent a signed policy.  
**Capacity control:** Limit early founding clients to 3–5 while we validate quality, fulfillment hours, retention, and pricing.

### Ideal client profile
- Owner-operated HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage-door, or related local service company in Grand Junction / Western Colorado.
- Customer/job value high enough for the $399 fee to have a plausible return.
- Handles local search inquiries; has capacity to take additional customers.
- Real customers, honest reviews, permission to manage the existing profile and website.
- Decision maker accessible and willing to compare business-relevant actions to a baseline.

**Poor fit:** business at full operational capacity, unwilling to disclose basic performance or grant access, high-volume low-ticket service without meaningful contribution margin, demands guaranteed Google placement, deceptive review requests, or requires extensive technical work within $399.

### What we sell and do not sell
We sell professional work and transparency: profile accuracy/maintenance, honest review-request infrastructure, basic directory consistency, a clearer mobile contact path, and monthly measurement. We do not sell Google Business Profile access (Google's tool is free), number-one rankings, phone-answering labor, unlimited web development, or guaranteed new revenue.

Price only after technical discovery.

## 3. First-contact and sales workflow

1. **Qualify:** Identify a real, relevant local company from public sources. Note whether the company is open, serves a defined geography, has an active owner/manager, capacity and economics.
2. **Observe:** Record 1–3 facts (e.g. hours inconsistent, contact button hidden on mobile). Label hypotheses as hypotheses; don't infer internal lost sales from public signals.
3. **Invite:** Offer a free, concise 3-point visibility check using public information. Request no credentials.
4. **Discover (20 min):** Ask which services are profitable, current lead sources, call handling, monthly capacity, estimated contribution per job, existing marketing arrangement, and desired next-quarter results.
5. **Prescribe:** Explain the top three gaps and why we prioritized them. Show the one-location standard scope, $399 founding rate, exclusions and client responsibilities.
6. **Contract:** Send proposal and statement of work with exact access expectations, privacy and measurement limitations, price, billing, termination, and written approval process. Obtain signed agreement and payment before implementation.
7. **Document:** Log lead, stage, source, disposition, next action in CRM/Airtable. Never claim an audit or lead has been delivered unless completed.

**Sales scripts should be honest:** “We can't guarantee ranking or revenue. We can document the problems, do agreed work, and report what changed.”

## 4. Onboarding and permissions

Collect business legal/public name; accurate address or service-area configuration; public phone and website; service area; hours; main service categories; brand assets; photo rights; business contacts; review-request approval; website stack/administrator approval; existing vendor agreements; reporting owner.

- Ask business to **invite Click & Mortar as GBP manager**. Business remains primary owner. Never request passwords or transfer primary ownership.
- Obtain written approval before material GBP edits, review responses, web edits, lead-tracking installation, or third-party connections.
- Capture baseline screenshots/data before changes; note which fields may not be available.
- Get explicit opt-in/valid consent and legal review before automated texting or recording; do not introduce such workflows in the founding plan by default.
- Keep credentials/access minimal. Avoid storing customer personal data in uncontrolled spreadsheets.
- Record issues that cannot be corrected because of ownership, verification or provider policies; escalation is separately scoped.
- Set the month's scheduled delivery and report date.

## 5. Fulfillment SOP: Day 1–30

**Days 1–3: Baseline**
- Confirm payment, SOW, permissions and public business facts.
- Capture Google profile name, categories, business description, services, phone, website, hours, service area, public reviews and photos.
- Check three high-value directory listings for inconsistent business facts.
- Walk the website on mobile as a customer: service explanation, trust, CTA, click-to-call, form behavior.
- Extract available GBP performance for agreed timeframe; annotate missing data or platform attribution limits.
- Rank fixes by business impact, confidence, risk and effort. Select three priorities.

**Days 4–10: Safe setup**
- Submit approved GBP corrections (never keyword stuff a business name, create false offices, or guess eligibility).
- Create approved neutral review link, QR graphic and honest request template usable for any genuine customer.
- Test mobile tap-to-call, navigation, and form. Implement one reversible, low-risk correction if appropriate.
- Send a brief written implementation update.

**Days 11–20: Priority execution**
- Inspect and submit up to two feasible high-priority listing corrections.
- Check client-submitted business photos/service facts; propose additional GBP improvements and obtain approval.
- Set review-response process; draft up to two replies as needed. No fabricated responses or private customer details.
- Retest site contact paths; note unsolved issues as future separately quoted work.

**Days 21–30: Reporting and renewal**
- Recheck profile state and requested corrections; note pending third-party approvals.
- Assemble client report with before/after items, factual metrics, observations and limits.
- Select next 1–3 priorities. Send next review date and client actions.
- Review fulfillment time/cost internally, flag scope creep and retention risk.

## 6. Monthly recurring SOP

**Week 1:** Review GBP accuracy, photos/hours/categories as relevant, and submit approved corrections. Record time and results.

**Week 2:** Inspect customer reviews and review-request link; draft responses within contracted cap. Recheck listing priorities.

**Week 3:** Address one small website contact-path issue where feasible, or document and separately scope the blocker. Submit up to two listing corrections as authorized.

**Week 4:** Export/record applicable GBP views, call-button clicks, website clicks, direction requests, search terms (where available), reviews, website inquiries if validly tracked, and confirmed bookings only if client supplies them. Deliver scorecard and agree next 1–3 priorities.

**Time target (internal planning assumption):** After setup, aim for 2–3 hours of standard work per client per month. Track the actual time. If two months exceed 4 hours for the $399 fee, revise process, remove inefficient work, or formally rescope/quote upgrades. Time targets are not a license to skip contracted deliverables.

## 7. Metrics, attribution and reports

Do not call Google Business Profile “calls” confirmed answered phone calls. Google’s metric is **call-button clicks**; website clicks are clicks on the listing link. Neither is a booked job. Mark source and date range beside every figure.

Include:
- **Outputs:** approved fixes made, QR review flow functioning, listings corrected/pending, website changes, reports delivered.
- **Leading indicators:** GBP interactions by type and period; reviews added and date; website form submissions/click-to-call if properly measured.
- **Commercial outcomes:** qualified leads, appointments booked, sold jobs, contribution margin, only if supplied by client with an agreed data definition.
- **Context:** seasonality, baseline length, paid activity, untracked calls, changed hours or campaigns.
- **Financial fit:** Ask owner to assess profit generated from newly tracked jobs; do not claim causality from correlation.

Never fabricate improvement, testimonials, SEO rank positions, lead counts, or case studies. Report “not available,” “pending,” and “no material change” honestly.

## 8. Compliance and risk

- Google local ranking is based on relevance, distance and prominence; no vendor can promise placement.
- Only ask real customers for truthful reviews with **neutral** language. Never provide incentives for Google reviews, review-gate, suppress legitimate feedback, purchase fake reviews, or ask only happy people.
- Clients own profile, website, analytics and lead data. Provide report/export and remove agency access on termination per contract.
- Google login role is manager whenever sufficient; maintain client ownership and a documented consent trail.
- Website form leads may contain personal information. Collect only necessary data; verify privacy notice, secure processing, and access permissions.
- Separate SMS/phone/AI work may require compliance with consent, call recording and telecom rules; obtain professional review before selling automated contact systems.
- No guarantees for ranking, count or timing of platform reviews/approvals, inquiries, appointments, or revenue.

## 9. Stage gates for expansion

**After 3 paying clients:** Can we deliver every promised item? Did owners find the scorecard useful? Are hours/costs sustainable? Any unexpected account/permission issues?

**After 5 paying clients / 60–90 days:** Calculate effective delivery contribution, payment collection, retention intent, evidence of business value, and repeatable bottlenecks. Consider raising standard pricing and introducing a higher tier only after learning.

**Advanced Customer Capture pilot (not yet a public product):** Interview owners about missed calls, inquiries and booking gaps. Propose one narrow separately quoted proof-of-concept using reliable existing tools. Measure response times and booked work with consent and integrations tested. Do not claim 24/7 answering or automatic booking until the system has been validated in real use.

## 10. Operating scoreboard

Every Friday record: qualified prospects contacted, owner conversations, audits sent, proposals, closes, active MRR, billed/invoiced cash, churn, average fulfillment hours, direct tool costs and top bottleneck. Every month review customer feedback and one avoidable repeated task to systematize.

**North Star rule:** Click & Mortar is the first capability factory. Build repeatable acquisition, measurable delivery, stable customer relationships, and managerial processes. Future software and acquisition strategy must be earned by evidence, not by distraction.
